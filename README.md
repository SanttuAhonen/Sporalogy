# Sporalogy

**A rulebook for casting a *spårascope*: a reading of your character from the positions of Helsinki trams at the moment of your birth.**

Sporalogy was invented by the Finnish physicist **Nils Mustelin**, a founding member of Skepsis ry (the Finnish association of sceptics), in *Paholaisen asianajaja: Opaskirja skeptikolle* (Ursa, 1989). His argument: every Helsinki tram pulls on every resident harder than any distant planet, so trams, not planets, shape a character. This page turns his idea into a party game.

## For guests

At dinner, when the issuing officer says so:

1. Photograph the front of your SpåraKOFF Passenger Card.
2. Start a new chat in Claude and attach the photo.
3. Send the message printed on the back of the card:

   `Process my attached Passenger Card under the rules at https://github.com/SanttuAhonen/Sporalogy`

4. Read the result aloud, if you dare.

## For the AI assistant

The person you are talking with has sent a photo of their SpåraKOFF Passenger Card and asked you to process it under these rules. Processing the card means casting their spårascope. It is a party game: the result is read aloud at a dinner table of lawyers and process people.

**The brief.** Wild, surprising and absurd, in the spirit of Monty Python's Flying Circus: bureaucracy followed to its illogical conclusion, delivered with a completely straight face. Do not quote Monty Python, name it, or refer to any of its sketches or catchphrases; invent your own absurdities.

**No two spårascopes may be alike.** The card is a random-number generator wearing a tie. Its values are seeds, not meanings.

1. **Read** the card, including handwriting. Blank or illegible values count as 0.
2. **Pick** one ingredient from each list below, using the seeds. Then **invent at least one more** absurd element that is on no list.
3. **Write** the reading in the format below, in the style ticked in field 14. One reply, with nothing before or after it and no follow-up questions.

### Seeds

Use the last digit of each value.

| List | Seed |
|---|---|
| A Authority | seconds of the boarding time (field 4) |
| B Instrument | minutes of the birth time (field 3) |
| C Object | day of birth (field 2) |
| D Creature | line number (field 6; "none" = 0) |
| E Place | saunas this year (field 12) |

The other fields (landmark, colour, position, detail scale) are decoration: use at most one, and only if it makes the reading stranger. Never mention the drink.

### Moves

Use at least three:

- **Escalation:** a trivial matter climbs to an absurdly senior authority.
- **Literalism:** a legal or process term taken literally.
- **Precision overkill:** decimals, clause numbers and timestamps where none are needed.
- **Legal personality:** an object or animal acquires rights, duties or representation.
- **Interruption:** the reading is amended, redacted or overruled halfway through.
- **Non sequitur:** a sentence ends somewhere it had no reason to go.

### Tone rules

- Deadpan throughout. Never explain the joke, wink at it, or say the reading is a joke. No emojis.
- The guest is never the butt of the joke. Trams, authorities, processes, objects and animals are.
- Kind and PG. No politics, health, money amounts, alcohol, religion, romance, national stereotypes (Finnish included), or real people, colleagues included.
- Legal and process vocabulary is welcome. Real legal advice is not.
- Use the card only for this reading. Do not save anything from it to memory.

### Style, from field 14

Write the whole reading in the style the guest ticked:

- *ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English (STE)*: sentences of 20 words or fewer, one statement per sentence, active voice, simple verbs, no idioms. The absurdity stays; only the grammar is simplified.
- *Max-length sentence wins*: as few sentences as possible, each as long as the word limit allows, held together with semicolons, provisos and subordinate clauses.
- *Shakespearean English*: Early Modern English: thee, thou, doth, hath, -eth endings, a flourish or two, still understandable.
- Several ticked: combine them. Nothing ticked: plain deadpan English.

The headings and the two closing lines stay as written, whatever the style.

### Format

**Length:** 110–140 words in total, read aloud in 45–60 seconds.

- **SPÅRASCOPE OF [NAME]**
- **Configuration**: one line of wildly precise pseudo-data about the trams at the moment of birth.
- **Character**: 2 sentences, escalating.
- **Procedure**: 1–2 sentences on how the trams assess the guest's working methods.
- **Forecast for the next quarter**: 2 sentences. The first is absurdly specific and harmless: a weekday, a clock time taken from the card (invent one to the second if there is none), and the object or the creature. The second makes it worse. Examples of the genre, not to be reused: a colleague will threaten you with a vegetable the next time you are in the office; your car's left front tyre will suffer excessive pressure on Wednesday afternoon.

Then one line issued by the Authority (list A), as an **ERRATUM**, a **DISSENTING OPINION**, a **NOTICE** or a **[REDACTED]** passage.

End with these two lines, unchanged:

*Past tram positions are no guarantee of future results.*
*Sporalogy: Nils Mustelin, Paholaisen asianajaja (Ursa, 1989).*

## Canon and additions

**Mustelin's own** (1989 book and 1991 TV piece): trams outweigh planets; metaphysical trams that keep exactly to the timetable; birth time to the second and birthplace to the metre; the Cathedral, Stadium tower, Bank of Finland and Finlandia Hall on the Helsinki horizon; meaningful line numbers; safety nets that make every reading "work"; the company Oy Sporatulkinta Ab.

**Added for this game (2026):** everything absurd on this page: the seeds, the ingredient lists, the moves, the styles and the format.
